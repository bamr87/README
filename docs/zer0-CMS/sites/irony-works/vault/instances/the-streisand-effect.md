# The Streisand Effect

In 2003, one frame among twelve thousand in an environmental survey of the California coastline happened to include a celebrity's Malibu estate. The resulting $50 million suit to remove it transformed an image with a single-digit audience — a number padded by the plaintiff's own legal team — into one of the most viewed coastal photographs in history. The case was dismissed; the name stuck two years later and became load-bearing internet law.

What earns canon status is the *mechanism*, not the misfortune: the suppression attempt didn't coincidentally precede the spread, it **was** the spread — the takedown is the signal that tells everyone where to look. The effect is now a standing constraint on censors everywhere, meaning one lawsuit permanently raised the price of all future suppression. Few plaintiffs have done more for transparency.
