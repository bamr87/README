# Thomas Midgley Jr.

One chemist, two environmental catastrophes, both introduced as safety improvements. Tetraethyl lead (1921) cured engine knock and salted the biosphere with neurotoxin; chlorofluorocarbons (circa 1930) replaced refrigerants that leaked and killed — with a compound so inert it survived all the way to the stratosphere, where it stopped being inert. The environmental historian J.R. McNeill's assessment — that no single organism in Earth's history had more impact on the atmosphere — is quoted in nearly every account, because the ledger supports it.

The [[zer0-CMS/sites/irony-works/vault/forms/cosmic|cosmic]] coda: after polio in 1940, Midgley engineered a rope-and-pulley rig to raise himself from bed — a modest, kind invention, built for an audience of one. In 1944 he was found entangled in its ropes, dead. The man whose planet-scale inventions harmed everyone *but* him was killed by the only one designed purely to help himself. The vault flags the occasional suggestion of suicide as unresolved; the irony stands either way, though it changes register.
