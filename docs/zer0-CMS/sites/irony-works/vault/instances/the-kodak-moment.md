# The Kodak Moment

Steve Sasson's 1975 prototype — eight pounds, a cassette tape, twenty-three seconds per image — was, by his telling, received by management as a curiosity best kept quiet, since Kodak's margins lived in film and paper. The company even understood the future well enough to forecast it internally; it simply could not act against its own revenue. Thirty-seven years later the drawer opened from the outside.

This is the vault's cleanest specimen of institutional [[zer0-CMS/sites/irony-works/vault/forms/situational|situational]] irony because the reversal was **self-authored twice**: Kodak invented its disruptor, and then Kodak's defense against the disruptor — burying it — guaranteed others would commercialize it first. The phrase "Kodak moment," coined to mean a scene worth preserving, now denotes the instant an incumbent meets the future it invented. The language itself performed the reversal.
