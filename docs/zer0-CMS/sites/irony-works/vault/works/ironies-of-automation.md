# Ironies of Automation

Bainbridge's 1983 paper is the rare engineering document that belongs in a literature vault, because its central finding is *formally* ironic — the title is a technical description. Designers automate what they can and leave the human as backstop for what they can't; therefore the human's role is, by construction, the residue of impossible tasks. Meanwhile the automation removes the daily practice that kept the operator sharp, so skill decays fastest precisely where it is most needed. The more reliable the system, the rarer the emergencies — and the less prepared the human who must handle them. Every clause reverses its own premise.

Four decades later the paper reads as prophecy for AI-assisted everything: code review by the deskilled, oversight of systems too capable to supervise casually.
