# The Gift of the Magi

The textbook's favorite [[zer0-CMS/sites/irony-works/vault/forms/situational|situational]] irony, and worth studying precisely because it is *engineered* — O. Henry builds a perfectly symmetric reversal the way a watchmaker builds an escapement. Each expectation causes the other's reversal; remove either sacrifice and the story is merely sweet.

What keeps it out of the [[zer0-CMS/sites/irony-works/vault/compost/the-merely-unfortunate|compost]] is the commentary clause: the outcome doesn't just thwart the plan, it *grades* it. The gifts fail as objects and succeed as evidence, and the narrator's closing judgment — that these two foolish children were the wisest givers — flips the reader's expectation one final time. A double reversal in under four thousand words. The vault notes, with professional respect, that the ending was reportedly drafted on a deadline.
