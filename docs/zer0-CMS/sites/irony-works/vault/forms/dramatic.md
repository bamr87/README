# Dramatic Irony

**Definition.** The audience knows what the character does not; every line the character speaks detonates twice.

**Mechanism.** An engineered awareness gap. The playwright hands the audience the ending, then makes them watch the character walk toward it with confidence. Tragic irony is its sharpest edge: the character's certainty is the mechanism of their destruction. The form proves that suspense is optional — Athens knew how Oedipus ended and packed the theater anyway.

**Commonly confused with.** Mere suspense or surprise. Surprise is the audience knowing *less* than the situation; dramatic irony is the audience knowing *more* than the character. The dread runs in opposite directions.
